About Me

I'm an Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's student in Cartography with structured mobility at Technical University of Munich (Germany), Vienna University of Technology (Austria), Dresden University of Technology (Germany), and the University of Twente (Netherlands). I hold a First-Class Honours degree in BSc Geomatic Engineering from the University of Mines and Technology, Ghana. My work sits at the intersection of geospatial data quality, remote sensing, spatial analysis, and geo / public health mapping. As a cartographer, I specialize in geovisualization, map design, and clear spatial data communication, applying these skills to develop interactive web maps using tools such as Leaflet.js and MapLibre GL JS.

Thematic Map Redesign: Hourly Wages and Salaries in Europe
Course Project · Geomedia Techniques, International Master's in Cartography
May 2026
Original Map
→
Redesign
Critiqued and redesigned a publicly shared thematic map of European salaries, identifying three core problems: an unclear data indicator and legend, a cluttered visual hierarchy, and an unjustified colour scale. Sourced the exact underlying Eurostat dataset (hourly wages and salaries, 2023) for an accurate, intuitive title, labelled "No data" countries in grey, removed competing elements like flags and dark backgrounds to bring focus to the thematic layer, and kept the blue-red diverging scale but clearly centred and labelled it on the EU average with each country's value shown.

Communicating Uncertainty in Flood Hazard Maps for Non-Expert Users
Seminar in Geosciences · International Master's in Cartography
Jun 2026
A structured literature review examining how uncertainty in flood hazard maps can be communicated to non-expert users without reducing comprehension or usability. Finds that intuitive visual design, plain legend language, familiar colour schemes, and testing with intended users are more effective than technical probability labels or dense uncertainty graphics.

Spatial Hotspots of Mpox Risks in the Democratic Republic of Congo: A Health-Zone Analysis
GeoHealth Course · University of Twente (ITC)
Feb 2026
A spatial statistics project analyzing Mpox case data across health zones in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Applies global and local spatial autocorrelation (Global and Anselin Local Moran's I) under multiple spatial weighting schemes - Queen contiguity, fixed distance bands, and k-nearest neighbors - to identify significant hotspots and inform public-health resource allocation. Supervised by Prof. Dr. Justine I. Blanford and Prof. Yanchao Cheng.
