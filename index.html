About Me

I am a geospatial researcher dedicated to building a more livable planet by bridging advanced spatial analysis with human-centered cartography. My research combines machine learning, remote sensing, and spatial data science with the design and rigorous evaluation of interactive geovisualizations. Ultimately, my goal is to transform complex geospatial data into accessible, actionable maps that improve environmental risk communication and public health outcomes.

I'm an Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's student in Cartography with structured mobility at Technical University of Munich (Germany), Vienna University of Technology (Austria), Dresden University of Technology (Germany), and the University of Twente (Netherlands). I hold a First-Class Honours degree in BSc Geomatic Engineering from the University of Mines and Technology, Ghana. My work sits at the intersection of geospatial data quality, remote sensing, spatial analysis, and geo / public health mapping. As a cartographer, I specialize in geovisualization, map design, and clear spatial data communication, applying these skills to develop interactive web maps using tools such as Leaflet.js and MapLibre GL JS.

Spatial Hotspots of Mpox Risks in Democratic Republic of the Congo: A Health-Zone Analysis
Geo-Health Project (Supervisor: Prof Justine Blanford and Prof Yanchao Cheng)
Oct 2025 – Feb 2026
  • Applied five spatial-weighting methods (Queen contiguity, fixed distance band, k-nearest neighbors) to map Mpox risk across 200 health zones in the DR Congo
  • Found significant positive spatial autocorrelation (p < 0.001) across all five methods, with k = 4 nearest neighbors (I = 0.259, z = 5.86) yielding the highest result
  • Findings supported prioritizing surveillance and diagnostic resources in Sankuru, Tshuapa, Sud-Kivu, and Maniema provinces, based on persistent hotspots identified across all methods

Two Thematic Maps, One Dataset: Electricity Access in West Africa
Course Project · Geomedia Techniques, International Master's in Cartography
Jun 2026
Print Magazine
+
Social Media
Two thematic maps were produced from the same World Bank electricity-access dataset for West Africa, each designed for a distinct audience and publication format. The print version, intended for a professional magazine (A5, landscape orientation), applies a six-class choropleth scale with formal typography and precise figures to present the data accurately and legibly. The social media version, formatted for a 1080×1080 square canvas, presents the same dataset through a simplified two-class classification, with direct title, and high-contrast colours.This project demonstrates different ways of visualizing and communicating the same dataset. Effective map design is not fixed, it adapts to the audience and the platform for which it is intended.

The Lake Volta of Ghana
GeoNode StoryMap · Cartographic Interface, International Master's in Cartography
Jun 2026
In 1965, the Akosombo Dam brought power to a newly independent Ghana, but it came at a massive cost. To build the dam - which still supplies about 85% of the country's electricity today - the government flooded the homes and histories of nearly 80,000 citizens across 700 villages. Built in GeoNode (WeMap), this interactive Geostory explores the ongoing tension between national development and environmental justice. By combining three immersive maps, a community carousel, and narrative text, it works as spatial data journalism to document what these communities sacrificed and the climate threats they still face today

RCMRD Project
Jul 2026
This dashboard maps Kenya's Protected and Conserved Areas (PCAs). It draws on the RCoE Geoportal's Kenya Biodiversity dataset. The dashboard combines an interactive map, summary indicators, and dynamic charts. It reports 352 PCAs with mapped boundaries, covering 80.7k square kilometres in total.
